_You built the app in Lovable. It works, people can sign up, and it still looks like every other Lovable app. A Lovable designer fixes how it looks and how it flows without throwing away what you built._

This is what that work involves, what it costs, how to tell a designer from a gig seller, and what you can fix yourself first.

## What does a Lovable designer do?

A Lovable designer takes an app that already runs and makes it look and work like a product someone chose to design. In practice that means four things:

1. **Sets the design rules.** One spacing scale, one type scale, one accent color, one corner style, written into project instructions so Lovable keeps them on every new screen.
2. **Redesigns the screens that matter.** Usually the landing page, sign up, the first screen after sign up, and the main task. That is where people decide to stay.
3. **Designs the states Lovable skipped.** Empty, loading, error and first run. Lovable builds the happy path; the rest is what makes an app feel finished.
4. **Hands it back in a form Lovable can use.** Either prompts and rules you paste in, Figma screens your developer builds from, or the changes made in the code directly.

What it is not: a rebuild. The data, the logic and the integrations stay. The design changes on top of them.

## Signs your Lovable app needs a designer

- The purple gradient and the glowing button are still there from the first prompt.
- Every screen picks its own spacing and the app feels uneven, but you cannot say why.
- People sign up and do not come back after the first session.
- You prompt "make it look more professional" and get a different version of the same thing.
- You are about to show it to investors or paying customers.

If two of these are true, the design is costing you signups you already paid for.

## What you can fix yourself first

Before you hire anyone, try this. It costs nothing and it fixes the most visible problems:

1. Paste a short set of design rules into project instructions: More, then Settings, then Instructions. [The rules and seven fix prompts are in this guide](/writing/lovable-app-looks-generic/).
2. Remove every gradient and glow, and pick one accent color.
3. Put spacing on one scale: 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64.
4. Run your site through [Slop Score](/slop-score/) to see what still reads as generated.

If the app looks consistent after that but still does not convert, the problem is the flow and the content, not the styling. That is the point where a designer earns their fee.

## Lovable designer, Lovable developer or Lovable partner?

These get mixed up, and they solve different problems.

- **A Lovable developer** fixes what does not work: bugs, integrations, the database, payments, performance.
- **A Lovable designer** fixes what does not look or feel right: layout, hierarchy, states, copy, consistency.
- **A Lovable partner** is an agency or freelancer in Lovable's own partner program, usually building a full app for you from scratch.

If your app works and looks generic, you need a designer. If it looks fine and breaks, you need a developer. If you have not built it yet, a partner or a designer and developer together.

## What to ask before you hire

- Show me an app built with Lovable, or another AI tool, that you redesigned. What changed after?
- Which three screens would you change first in my app, and why?
- How do you hand the work back: prompts, Figma or code?
- How do you keep Lovable from undoing the design on the next prompt?
- What happens to my data and logic while you work?

A good answer to the fourth question tells you more than a portfolio does. If the designer does not mention project instructions or a rules file, the next prompt will undo their work.

## Red flags

- A fixed number of revisions instead of a fixed outcome
- Screenshots of templates instead of real apps
- No mention of empty, loading or error states
- A plan to rebuild the app from scratch when it already works

## Questions people ask

### Can a designer work inside my Lovable project?

Yes. You can invite a collaborator to the project. Many designers prefer to design in Figma first and then apply the changes through prompts and project instructions, so you approve the design before anything changes in the app.

### How long does a Lovable redesign take?

One to two weeks for the rules and the key screens of a small app. A larger product with many flows takes two to four weeks. A designer who promises everything in two days is changing the theme, not the design.

### Will the redesign break my app?

It should not. The design sits on top of the logic and the data. Ask for the changes on a copy of the project or a branch, and test the main flows before you publish.

### Do I need a designer if I use a DESIGN.md?

A [DESIGN.md](/writing/design-md-template/) gets you consistency: every screen follows the same rules. A designer decides what the rules should be and which screens and states the product needs. Start with the file; hire when consistency is not enough.
