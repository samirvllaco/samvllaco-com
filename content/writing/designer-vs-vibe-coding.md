I'm a designer, and I build my own products with Claude and Cursor. So for me this isn't designer vs AI. It's which decisions you can make yourself, and which ones you'd rather hand over.

## What AI tools do well, and where they stop

AI is fast. It can ship a screen in 30 seconds. For a prototype, an internal tool or an idea you're still testing, that speed is worth more than polish.

The catch is how it works. AI predicts the most likely interface, and the most likely interface is the most common one. So you get the average: a purple gradient, rounded cards, three feature boxes.

It also builds the happy path, where data exists and nothing breaks. Empty, loading and error states get skipped unless you ask for them.

None of this is a bug. Give the tool specific decisions and it follows them. The real question is who makes those decisions: you, or a designer.

## Vibe code it yourself when

- You have no users yet, and you're testing whether anyone wants this.
- It's a prototype for a pitch, or an internal tool only your team sees.
- Your whole budget is less than a designer's smallest project.
- The product is a few screens with standard patterns: a form, a list, a settings page.
- You can say what you want in exact values: a spacing scale, four text sizes, one accent color.
- You're a developer who wants rules to follow, not a redesign.

In these cases a designer is the wrong first spend. Ship it, see if people use it, then decide.

## Hire a designer when

- People sign up, look around and leave, and you can't say why.
- You've typed "make it look better" five times and keep getting the same purple cards.
- You sell to businesses, or you handle money or health data, and looking unfinished costs you trust.
- The product has hard screens: onboarding, dashboards with real data, tables with 3,000 rows.
- You're about to launch or raise, and people will open your product next to funded ones.
- Your team ships every week and every new screen looks a little different.
- Your hours are worth more with customers than on prompting spacing.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need a designer if I use AI to build my app?

Not for a first version. AI tools are enough to test an idea. You need a designer, or at least a designer's rules, when people have to trust the product enough to pay for it.

### Can AI replace a UI/UX designer?

For standard screens, AI does much of the production work. It doesn't know your users, and it gives you the most common layout unless you hand it specific decisions. Someone still has to make those decisions.

### How much does it cost to fix the design of a vibe-coded app?

With me there are four prices. The ebook is $12.99 if you fix it yourself. A recorded review is $49 or $99. A design project starts at $5,000, with a fixed price in writing before work starts.

### Will a designer make me rebuild my AI-built app?

Not when I do it. The structure stays. What changes is spacing, type, color, states and copy, which is where an AI-built app gives itself away.
