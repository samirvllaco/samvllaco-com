_One dashboard screen can be a week of work. A dashboard product can be three months. Both get called "a dashboard" in the brief._

That is why quotes for dashboard design are so far apart. The word covers everything from one overview screen to a full analytics product with tables, drill-downs, filters and permissions. Before you ask what it costs, work out which one you are buying.

## The short answer

Using my own published prices as the example:

- **One screen, redesigned:** about one sprint week, $1,500.
- **A full dashboard product,** with many views, a component library and every state: fixed price, in the $5,000 to $25,000 range my larger product work runs in.

For comparison, Clutch reports that most UX agencies charge $25 to $49 an hour and that UX projects there are typically more than $10,000. An agency quote for a full dashboard product will usually start above that.

## Six things that decide the price

**1. The number of different views.** An overview, a list, a detail page, a report builder and a settings area are five designs. Five tabs that share one layout with different data are closer to one.

**2. How many kinds of user.** An admin, a manager and a read-only viewer may each need a different home screen. Every role that sees something different adds work.

**3. The tables.** A simple list is quick. A table with sorting, saved filters, bulk actions, inline editing and a side panel is a small product by itself. I wrote up what a good one needs in [data table design](/writing/data-table-design/).

**4. The charts.** Standard lines and bars from your charting library cost little. Custom visuals, such as a funnel with drop-off or a map, take design time and build time.

**5. The states.** Empty, loading, error, no permission and no results, for every card and every table. A quote that skips these is cheaper and so is the result.

**6. What exists already.** A design system and a clear list of metrics cut the work a lot. Starting from nothing, with the team still arguing about which numbers matter, adds a week of deciding before any designing.

## What pushes the cost up without making the dashboard better

- **Designing every chart anyone might want.** Start with the questions people ask weekly. Add the rest when someone asks.
- **Custom everything.** A custom date picker, a custom table and custom charts look special and cost three times as much to build and maintain.
- **No sample data.** A designer working from guesses will redo the layout when the real numbers arrive. Send an export on day one.
- **Five people signing off.** One decision maker. Everyone else gives input to that person.

## How to scope yours in ten minutes

Write down:

1. Who opens the dashboard and how often.
2. The three questions each kind of user opens it to answer.
3. A list of the views you think you need.
4. Which of those have tables people act on.
5. Whether you have a component library today.

Send that with a sample export and any designer can give you a real number. The [design cost calculator](/tools/design-cost-calculator/) will give you a rough one in a minute.

## Redesign or rebuild

A redesign costs less than most teams fear, because it usually does not mean a rebuild. Most of the improvement comes from hierarchy, grouping and table design, and those fit the components you already have. If your dashboard works but nobody can read it, budget for a redesign of the main screens first and see what that changes.

## Questions people ask

### How much does it cost to design a SaaS dashboard?

From about $1,500 for one redesigned screen to $25,000 or more for a full dashboard product with many views, tables and states. The number of different views and the complexity of the tables decide most of it.

### How long does a dashboard design take?

One screen is often a week. A full product is longer and is scoped in writing before work starts.

### Is it cheaper to redesign a dashboard than to rebuild it?

Usually much cheaper. Most gains come from hierarchy and table design, which fit the components you already have.

### What should I send a designer to get a dashboard quote?

Who uses it, the questions they open it to answer, a list of views, and a sample export of real data. With those, a quote takes one call.
