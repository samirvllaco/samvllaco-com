_Short answer: Hire a freelance product designer when the work fits one senior person: one product, one decision maker and a scope you can describe in a paragraph. Hire a design agency when you need several specialists working at the same time, a fixed deadline one person can't cover, or a vendor your company can formally approve._

I'm a freelance product designer, so I have a side in this. I'll still tell you when an agency is the better call. A bad fit wastes your money and my time.

## The one difference that explains the rest

A freelancer is one person. An agency is a team with a process around it.

Everything else follows from that. One person costs less, starts sooner and is easier to talk to. A team covers more skills, does more at once and doesn't stop when someone gets sick.

So the question isn't which one is better. It's how much work you have, and how many kinds.

## Freelance designer vs agency at a glance

- **Cost:** Freelance product designer: You pay for one person's time. Small projects are possible: one flow, one audit; Design agency: You pay for a team and the people who manage it. Minimums are higher.
- **Speed:** Freelance product designer: Quick to start and quick to decide. Capped by one person's hours; Design agency: Slower to kick off. Can put several people on the work at once.
- **Who you talk to:** Freelance product designer: The person doing the design; Design agency: Often a project or account manager. Ask who joins your calls.
- **Range of skills:** Freelance product designer: Deep in a few areas. The rest comes from you or from people they bring in; Design agency: Research, UX, UI, brand, motion and often development in one place.
- **Risk:** Freelance product designer: One person. No backup if they're sick or overbooked; Design agency: Cover if someone leaves. Check that the seniors who pitched you do the work.
- **After launch:** Freelance product designer: You own the files. Follow-up depends on their calendar; Design agency: Retainers are common. The team can outlast any one person.

## What UI/UX design costs

There is no single honest market number, so here are two real ones.

Clutch, a directory of agency reviews, says UX projects on its platform are typically more than $10,000. Its average UX agency project costs $84,973, and the typical timeline is 10 months (pricing guide updated 21 September 2026). That average includes large, long projects. It isn't a quote for yours.

My own prices: projects start at $5,000, with a fixed price in writing before work starts, or a $2,500 trial week first.

These two aren't like for like. An agency quote often includes research, project management or development that a freelance quote leaves out. Compare scope first, then price. Ask both for a written scope so you can see the difference.

## Pick a freelance product designer when

- You have one product and one person who can say yes or no.
- The job is clear: an onboarding flow, a dashboard, a design system or a [UX audit](/services/ux-audit/).
- You already have developers. You need files they can build from, not a build team.
- You want the senior person on every call and in every file.
- Your budget is a few thousand dollars, and you'd rather spend it on design than on coordination.
- Plans change every week and you want to change scope with a message.

If most of those are true, one senior designer is enough. Extra people would add meetings, not speed.

## Pick a design agency when

- You need research, brand, product design and development moving at the same time.
- The deadline is fixed and the work is more than one person can finish by then.
- Your company needs a vendor that can pass procurement, security reviews and insurance checks.
- You need guaranteed cover, including holidays and sick days.
- Nobody on your team has time to brief and review a designer, and you want a project manager to run it.
- It's a rebrand, a new website and a product redesign, all at once.

If two or more of those are true, go with an agency. You'll pay more, and you'll get what you're paying for.

## The middle path

You don't have to pick one forever.

- **Test small first.** Give a freelancer or an agency one flow or one audit before a big contract. You learn how they work before the big money moves.
- **Split the work.** Put a senior freelancer on the core product, and a specialist studio on the one thing they don't do, like a full rebrand.
- **Switch by stage.** A freelancer while the product is small. An agency or an in-house team once you're shipping on five fronts at once.
- **Not sure what's wrong yet?** Start with a [recorded UI/UX review](/fix-ai-slop-ui/#options) from $49. You'll know what to fix before you hire anyone.

## Questions people ask

### Is a freelance designer cheaper than an agency?

Usually, for the same scope. You pay for one person and no management layer. An agency quote often covers more, like research or development, so compare what's included before you compare price.

### Should I hire a freelance UX designer or an agency for a SaaS MVP?

If you have developers and one decision maker, a senior freelancer is usually enough for an MVP. If you need design, brand and the build delivered together by a fixed date, an agency fits better.

### What is the biggest risk of hiring a freelance designer?

It's one person. If they get sick or overbooked, there is no backup. Lower the risk with a written scope, a fixed price, a shared Figma file you can open any day and a small first project.

### Can I use a freelancer and an agency together?

Yes. A simple split is a freelancer on the core product and an agency or studio on a rebrand or a large build. Make sure one person on your side owns the decisions.
