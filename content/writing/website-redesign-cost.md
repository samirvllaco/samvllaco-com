_A website redesign costs what the number of different pages, the content and the build add up to. With me a redesign starts at $5,000 at a fixed price, and a week of design work is about $2,500. Below is how to work out your own number in ten minutes._

Published prices for a website redesign run from a few hundred dollars to six figures, which tells you nothing. The spread comes from five things you can count yourself. Count them, and you can tell a fair quote from a padded one.

## The five things that set the price

1. **How many page types.** Not how many pages. Forty blog posts share one layout. A home page, a pricing page, a feature page, an about page and a contact page are five layouts. Design time follows layouts.
2. **Who writes the words.** A redesign with the same copy is a new coat of paint. New structure and new headlines take time, from you or from the designer.
3. **Whether it includes the build.** Design in Figma is one price. Design plus a working site is roughly that again, depending on the platform.
4. **Brand work.** New colors and type on an existing logo is small. A new identity adds about a week.
5. **What has to survive.** Search rankings, redirects, a CMS full of content, forms wired to a CRM. Moving these safely is real work that cheap quotes leave out.

## Rough ranges by size

Using my own pricing as the yardstick:

- **One landing page:** a few days of design. It fits inside a $2,500 trial week with time left for the mobile version and the states.
- **A small marketing site, about five page types:** one to two weeks of design. This is where projects from $5,000 start.
- **A larger site, ten or more page types with a CMS:** three to six weeks of design, plus the build.

## Redesign, refresh or rebuild?

- **Refresh:** same structure, new look. Colors, type, spacing, images. The cheapest option, and enough when the site explains the product well and just looks dated.
- **Redesign:** new structure and new copy on the pages that matter. The right choice when people land and do not understand what you do, or do not act.
- **Rebuild:** a new platform as well. Only when the current one is the problem: too slow, impossible to edit, or no longer supported.

Most sites I am asked to redesign need the middle one, on three to five pages. The other thirty pages need the refresh.

## How to keep the cost down

- **Redesign the pages people land on.** Check your analytics. A handful of pages usually get most of the visits.
- **Bring the content.** Real text, real screenshots and real customer names before design starts.
- **Decide who approves.** One person. Design by committee adds weeks.
- **Ask for a fixed price** as "these page types, these states, by this date, for this total".

## What a cheap redesign leaves out

A low quote is often a quote for the home page on a desktop screen. Ask whether it includes the phone layout, the empty and error states of forms, redirects from old addresses, accessibility checks and the handoff to whoever builds it. Each one left out comes back as a cost later.

## Questions people ask

### How much does a website redesign cost?

It depends on the number of page types, the content and the build. With me, one landing page fits in a $2,500 week and a small marketing site starts at $5,000 at a fixed price.

### How long does a website redesign take?

One to two weeks of design for a small site, three to six for a larger one. The build comes on top, and content is the most common cause of delay.

### Is it cheaper to redesign or to start again?

Redesigning is usually cheaper, because the content, the platform and the search rankings stay. Start again only when the platform itself is holding you back.

### Will a redesign hurt my search rankings?

It can if addresses change without redirects or if pages that rank are removed. Keep the addresses, redirect the ones that must change and keep the content that brings visits.
