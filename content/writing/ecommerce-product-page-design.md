_Ecommerce product page design comes down to answering five questions before the shopper has to ask: what is it, what does it cost in total, will it suit me, when will it arrive and can I send it back. A page that answers all five above the buy button sells more than a prettier one that hides them._

A product page is where the decision happens. Most of the ones I review lose the sale on small things: a price that changes in the cart, a size guide behind three taps, a buy button that scrolls away on a phone. Here is what goes on the page and in what order.

## The first screen

On a phone, before any scrolling, the shopper should see:

1. **The product photo,** large, with dots or thumbnails showing there are more.
2. **The name,** in plain words. What it is, then the variant.
3. **The price,** with any saving shown as a real amount.
4. **The rating and the number of reviews,** linked to the reviews.
5. **The buy button,** or the option picker that leads to it.

If one of these is below the fold on a small phone, move something decorative out of the way.

## Make the choice easy

- **Show options as buttons, not a dropdown.** Sizes, colors and pack sizes are easier to compare when they are all visible.
- **Mark what is out of stock** on the option itself, before the tap.
- **Subscribe or buy once, side by side.** If you sell subscriptions, show both with their real prices. In [a supplements store I designed](/work/supplements-store/), the two options sit next to each other, so the saving is clear and nobody feels tricked into a plan.
- **Change the photo with the option.** Picking blue should show blue.

## No surprises on price

The fastest way to lose a sale is a total that grows in the cart.

- Show **delivery cost and time** on the product page, or the amount needed for free delivery.
- Show **the saving** as money, not only a percentage.
- In the cart, **list every line** of the total: items, discount, delivery, tax. Repeat the total on the button.

## Answer the doubts

Under the first screen, in this order:

1. **What it does for me,** in three to five short points. Benefits, then the details.
2. **Details people check:** ingredients, materials, dimensions, what is in the box.
3. **Delivery and returns,** in two lines, in plain words.
4. **Reviews,** with photos where you have them and a way to filter by rating.
5. **Questions,** from real customer emails.
6. **Related products,** last. Above the reviews they pull people away from buying.

## The buy button

- **One filled button on the page.** "Add to cart" in the accent color. Everything else is an outline or a link.
- **Keep it reachable.** On phones, a bar with the price and the button stays at the bottom once the main button scrolls away.
- **Confirm the add** without leaving the page: a short message and the cart count going up.
- **Loading and error states.** Tapping twice should not add two.

## Photos

- At least four: the product alone, in use, the scale next to something familiar, and the detail people ask about.
- The same background and crop across the store, so a category page reads as one set.
- Fast. Size images to the screen and load the ones below the fold later. A slow first photo is a blank product.

## After the order

Almost every buyer reads the page after checkout. Use it: say what happens next, when it arrives and how to change the order. In the supplements store, a daily plan and refill reminders give people a reason to come back.

## Questions people ask

### What should an ecommerce product page include?

Photos, a plain name, the price with any saving, the rating, the options, the buy button, delivery and returns, the details people check, reviews and questions. On a phone the first five should fit on the first screen.

### How many images should a product page have?

At least four: the product alone, in use, something showing its size and a close view of the detail people ask about.

### Where should the add to cart button go?

Directly under the price and options on the first screen, and in a bar fixed to the bottom on phones once that button has scrolled out of view.

### Should related products be on the product page?

Yes, below the reviews and questions. Placed higher, they take attention away from the product the shopper came for.
