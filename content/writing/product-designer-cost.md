_Two quotes for the same app can be $4,000 and $35,000. Both can be fair. Here is what you are paying for in each._

Most pricing articles are written by agencies that do not say what they charge. I will say where my own projects start and use that as one of the examples, next to the public figures from [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/product-designers/cost/), [Clutch](https://clutch.co/agencies/ui-ux/pricing) and [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/ui-ux-design-cost/).

## The short answer

- **Hourly:** $20 to $50 an hour on Upwork for product designers. Most UX agencies on Clutch charge $25 to $49 an hour. Senior specialists sit above both.
- **One flow or one feature:** roughly $1,000 to $5,000.
- **A full SaaS product, first version:** $5,000 to $25,000 with a senior freelancer. Clutch says UX projects there are typically more than $10,000, and agency quotes run well past that.
- **In-house:** Figma's cost guide cites a US median salary of $104,000 a year for digital designers, before benefits, tools and the months it takes to hire.

The ranges are wide because "design my app" is not one product. The rest of this page is what moves the number.

## The four ways designers charge

**By the hour.** Good for small fixes and second opinions: a pricing page cleanup, a file review, a few screens before launch. Bad for anything large, because you carry all the risk of it running long. I charge $50 an hour, billed in one-hour blocks.

**By the week.** A design sprint: one focused week on one problem. Redesign the onboarding, or take a feature from flow to final screens. You know the cost before it starts and you can add weeks. Mine is $1,500 a week.

**By the month.** A retainer, for teams that ship every sprint and need a designer on call. Mine is $5,000 a month, with a weekly call, and you can pause or cancel any month.

**Fixed price.** A whole website, app or brand, scoped on a call and priced in writing before work starts. My projects start at $5,000, or with a $2,500 trial week.

If you are comparing quotes, convert them all to the same thing: what do I get, by when, for how much in total. An hourly rate tells you almost nothing on its own.

## What moves the price

**The number of screens that are really different.** A settings page and a billing page share most of their parts. A dashboard, an editor and an onboarding flow share almost nothing. Count the unique layouts, not the pages.

**How many states each screen has.** Empty, loading, error, success, no permission, on a phone. A designer who quotes low is often quoting the happy path only. Ask.

**Whether the flows exist yet.** If you know exactly what each screen does, you are buying UI. If someone has to work out what the product should do, you are buying product design, and it costs more because it is worth more.

**Research.** Talking to users, testing a prototype, reading your analytics. It adds days. It also stops you building the wrong thing, which is the most expensive design mistake there is.

**A design system.** Components with every state and tokens for color, type and spacing take longer the first time. They make every screen after that faster, for your designers and your developers.

**Who does the work.** An agency quote includes a project manager, an account lead and a margin. A freelancer quote is one person's time. Neither is wrong. They are different products.

**Where the designer is.** Rates follow the cost of living. Figma's guide notes that designers in major US tech hubs charge more than those in lower-cost regions. I am in Tirana, which is part of why a senior week costs $1,500.

**Speed.** A hard deadline next week costs more than the same work next month.

**Under $100.** No designer, but real help. [My book](/fix-ai-slop-ui/) is $12.99: 27 prompts you paste into Claude, Cursor, Lovable or v0 to fix the design yourself. A recorded review of your product is $49 for 30 minutes, or $99 for the longer one with a scorecard and custom prompts.

**$500 to $1,500.** A few hours of fixes on a live product, or one focused week on one flow. Enough to fix the screen that is losing people.

**$1,500 to $5,000.** Two or three sprint weeks. A full onboarding or a dashboard.

**$5,000 to $25,000.** A full product: flows, every screen and state, a component library and a clickable prototype. With a senior freelancer this is weeks, not months.

**$25,000 and up.** Agency territory, or a long engagement. You are paying for a team, more process and usually research. Worth it when the product is large and several people need to work in parallel.

## Three ways to pay less without getting less

1. **Bring real content.** Real copy, real data, real screenshots of what exists. Designers lose days inventing placeholder content and then redoing the layout when the real content arrives.
2. **Name one decision maker.** Design by committee doubles the rounds. One person signs off.
3. **Buy the smallest useful thing first.** One flow, one week. You get a finished piece and you learn whether to buy the rest from the same person.

## Cheap design is the expensive kind

The $300 app design exists. You get screens that look fine in a screenshot and fall apart in the build: no states, no system, no thought about what happens on a phone. Your developers fill the gaps on the fly, the product ships inconsistent, and six months later you pay for the redesign you were trying to avoid.

The opposite mistake is just as common. A $40,000 agency engagement for a product that has not found its first ten customers is a lot of polish on a guess.

Spend in proportion to what you know. Little evidence, small budget, one flow. Real users and real numbers, then the full product.

## Questions people ask

### How much does it cost to hire a product designer per hour?

Upwork lists product designers at $20 to $50 an hour and Clutch reports most UX agencies at $25 to $49 an hour. Senior specialists charge more. I charge $50 an hour.

### How much does it cost to design a SaaS app?

A first version with a senior freelancer usually lands between $5,000 and $25,000, depending on the number of unique screens, the states and whether the flows are already decided. Agency quotes are higher.

### Is a freelance designer cheaper than an agency?

Usually, for the same scope, because you are paying for one person and no layers. Figma's guide gives the example of $4,000 against $35,000 for what reads like the same deliverables.

### Should I pay hourly or fixed price?

Hourly for small, open-ended fixes. Fixed price for anything you can scope, because the designer carries the risk of it running long, not you.
