_Your website looks AI-generated when it shows the defaults every AI builder reaches for: a purple gradient, glowing buttons, three identical feature cards, emoji icons and copy that could describe any product. Here are the 10 signs to check, a free test, and the fix for each._

Visitors do not run a checklist. They get a feeling in the first second, and that feeling decides whether they read your headline. The 10 signs below are what produces it.

## The 10 signs a website looks AI-generated

1. **A purple, violet or pink gradient** behind the hero or on the headline text.
2. **Glow.** Neon shadows around buttons and cards, or a blurred color blob in a corner.
3. **Three feature cards in a row,** each with an icon, a two-word title and one vague line.
4. **Emoji used as icons,** often a rocket, a sparkle and a lightning bolt.
5. **Uneven spacing.** The gap between sections and cards changes from one block to the next.
6. **Too many grays** and text sizes, so nothing looks more important than anything else.
7. **Hype copy.** "Revolutionize", "seamless", "unlock", "supercharge", "next-level".
8. **Buttons that say "Get started" or "Learn more"** everywhere, with two or three filled buttons on one screen.
9. **Stock or AI-made images** that show no real product: abstract 3D shapes, smiling people at laptops.
10. **Missing details.** No real numbers, no real customer names, a footer full of links that go nowhere.

One or two of these is normal. Four or more and people file the site under "generated" before they read it.

## Why AI-built websites end up looking the same

AI builders are trained on a great many websites and pick the most likely design for each part. Most likely means most common, so the tools land on the same defaults: the same component library, the same gradient, the same card layout. None of it is wrong. It is just everyone's, so it says nothing about you.

## How to fix each sign

- **Gradient and glow.** Remove both. Pick one accent color and use it only for the main button, links and focus.
- **Three feature cards.** Replace one row with something only your product can show: a real screenshot, a number, a short before and after.
- **Emoji icons.** Use one line-icon set at one size and stroke weight, or no icons.
- **Spacing.** One scale, for example 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, and nothing between.
- **Grays and sizes.** Two grays for text, one for borders. Four or five text sizes in total.
- **Hype copy.** Say what the product does, for whom, and one result with a number.
- **Buttons.** One filled button per screen, labelled with a verb and an object: "Create invoice", "Book a demo".
- **Images.** Show the product. A plain screenshot beats any abstract render.
- **Details.** Real numbers, real names with permission, and a footer where every link works.

To do most of this in one pass, paste this into Lovable, Cursor or Claude Code:

```
Remove every gradient, glow and emoji icon from this site. Use one accent color for primary buttons, links and focus only. Put all spacing on this scale: 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. Use two grays for text and one for borders. Keep one filled button per screen and label each button with a verb and an object. Do not change the layout or the content of any section. List the files you changed.
```

## Does it matter if a website looks AI-generated?

It matters when you are asking for trust: a payment, a sign up, a sales call. People read a generic look as low effort, and they assume the product behind it got the same effort. A site that looks chosen, even a plain one, tells them someone cared about the details.

It does not mean you should hide that you used AI. Almost everyone does. The goal is a site that looks like your product and no one else's.

## Questions people ask

### How can I tell if a website was made with AI?

Look for the defaults: a purple gradient, glow, three identical feature cards, emoji icons, uneven spacing and vague copy. No single sign proves it, and plenty of human-made sites have them too. What you are really spotting is a site where nobody made the design decisions.

### Is there a free AI-generated website checker?

Yes. Slop Score, my free [AI slop checker](/ai-slop-checker/), checks a live URL and gives a 0 to 100 score with the top fixes. The score and top three fixes are free.

### Does Google penalize AI-generated websites?

Google's guidance is about content quality, not how a page was built. It asks site owners to review and fact-check AI-generated content, and its spam policies cover pages made at scale with no value for users. A generic look is a trust problem with visitors, not a ranking rule.

### Can I fix it without a designer?

Usually, yes. The prompt above and a rules file remove most of the signs. A designer helps when the site is consistent and still does not convert, because then the problem is the message and the flow.
