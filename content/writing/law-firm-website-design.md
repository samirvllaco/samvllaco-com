_Law firm website design has one job: get a person who needs a lawyer to call or send their case. That takes a short headline, the attorney's face, proof in the first screen and a phone number that is never more than a thumb away._

Someone looking for a lawyer has usually had a bad week. They are on a phone, comparing three firms, and they give each site a few seconds. Most law firm sites spend those seconds on a long headline, a stock photo of a gavel and a menu with fourteen items. Here is what I put on the page instead, based on a homepage I redesigned for a personal injury firm in South Texas.

## What the first screen needs

1. **A short headline that says who you help.** "Injured in a crash? We handle it from here." A visitor should know they are in the right place before they scroll.
2. **The attorney.** A real portrait, with the name. People hire a person, and a group photo in a lobby says less than one face.
3. **Proof.** A review rating with the number of reviews, or the amount recovered, directly under the headline.
4. **Two ways to act.** A free case review and a call. Both visible without scrolling, on a phone too.

On the Texas project the old page opened with a long headline and a group photo. The new one leads with a short headline, the attorney, a review badge and those two actions. [The case study](/work/law-firm-website/) shows both side by side.

## What comes after the first screen

Give each section one purpose and one heading. The order I use:

- **Results and awards,** directly under the hero.
- **Practice areas as cards,** each with one line in plain words and a link to its own page.
- **How it works,** in three steps: tell us what happened, we review it, you decide.
- **The attorney,** in a few sentences that sound like a person.
- **Reviews,** with names where you have permission.
- **Offices,** with addresses and a map.
- **Questions,** starting with what it costs to talk to you.
- **A final call to action.**

That redesign went from 11 sections to 17. A longer page was the better page, because every section answered one question a worried person has.

## Five things that lose the call

- **A phone number that is an image** or that does not dial when tapped.
- **An intake form with twelve fields.** Ask for a name, a phone number and what happened. The rest comes on the call.
- **Legal language in the headline.** "Zealous representation" means nothing to someone who was hit by a truck.
- **A slider in the hero.** Nobody waits for the third slide.
- **A site that reads only in English** when a large share of your clients speak another language. The Texas page has a Spanish block for that reason.

## Make it work on a phone first

Many of these visits are on a phone. Check four things on yours:

1. The phone number and the case review button are visible on the first screen.
2. Buttons are at least 44 pixels tall.
3. Text is 16 pixels or larger and has strong contrast.
4. The page loads fast on a mobile connection. A large video in the hero is the usual reason it does not.

## Accessibility and the rules of your bar

A law firm site should be usable with a keyboard and a screen reader: labelled form fields, real buttons, text contrast of 4.5 to 1. The free [contrast checker](/tools/contrast-checker/) tests your colors in a minute.

Advertising rules for lawyers differ by state and country, and they cover things like results, testimonials and the word "specialist". I design the page; your firm confirms the wording against its own bar's rules before it goes live.

## Questions people ask

### What should a law firm website include?

A short headline that says who you help, the attorney's portrait, proof such as reviews or results, a phone number and a short case review form on the first screen. Then practice areas, how it works, reviews, offices, questions and a final call to action.

### How many pages does a law firm website need?

A home page, one page per practice area, an attorney page, a contact page and the legal pages. Practice area pages matter most for search, because people search for the problem they have.

### How long does a law firm website redesign take?

A homepage redesign takes one to two weeks of design. A full site with practice area pages takes three to six, depending on how many areas you cover and who writes the content.
